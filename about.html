How I work

Clarity before complexity.

Start with the real workflow Map what the team actually does, including the workarounds.
Connect what already works Keep useful tools and remove the gaps between them.
Leave a system people can run Clear ownership, practical controls and a proper handover.

Most businesses don't have a technology problem. They have a joining-up problem.

The work is already getting done. It just gets done in nine different places that don't talk to each other. The same figure gets typed into a sheet, a form and an invoice. An enquiry sits in someone's inbox over the weekend. And the owner finds out how last month went somewhere in the middle of this one.

That gap is what I build for. Not another tool to add to the pile, but the layer that ties the pile together, so the business becomes something you can actually see and steer.

I'm not precious about the stack. I work across the major AI platforms and lean on prompt-led, AI-assisted and no-code or low-code development, because what matters is a working system this quarter, not a rebuild next year. Where AI earns its place, it takes the repetitive work, and a person still approves anything that counts.

Every engagement starts the same way: I learn how your organisation actually runs. Not the org chart version, the real thing, including the workaround everyone's too busy to mention.

Approach

Automating a broken process just gets you the wrong answer faster. So the tooling comes last, on purpose.

The objective

We do not build AI because it is popular.
We build it because the problem is real.

Too much AI is designed to impress during a demonstration. We would rather build the practical, unglamorous systems that quietly make a business work better every day.

The kind of AI that:

  • Removes hours of repetitive work every week
  • Gives you a clear and honest view of your numbers
  • Helps your team respond faster and operate consistently
  • Connects with the tools and workflows you already use
  • Continues to perform as your business grows

The goal is not to add more technology.

The goal is to reduce complexity, improve decisions and build systems that remain dependable when the business doubles.
